Jar of Beads

CCSS 7.SP.A.1 · Samples support inferences only when they are representative and big enough

1 · Predict

A jar holds 1,000 beads, some red, the rest white. You pull out a handful of 10 beads and count the red ones. If you did that 200 times, the share of red in your handfuls would be…

2 · Play

The jar’s true red share is hidden. Choose a handful size, then draw many handfuls. Each bar in the chart counts how many handfuls had that share of red.

A school surveys 8 students and finds 6 prefer a new lunch menu. The principal announces “75% of students love it.” What does the beads chart suggest?

Teacher note

Standard: 7.SP.A.1 – understand that statistics can be used to gain information about a population by examining a sample; generalisations are valid only if the sample is representative; random sampling tends to produce representative samples and supports valid inferences.

Target misconception: “A sample mirrors the population, whatever its size” (belief in the law of small numbers).

Ask: What happens to the width of the chart when the handful grows? Does the centre move? How big would a handful need to be before you trust it?

Extend: change the hidden share in the code; discuss bias (what if reds are heavier and sink?).

Limit: samples here are random by construction; the simulation does not show biased sampling.
